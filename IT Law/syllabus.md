## Course Structure & Modules (End-Term)

### Module 5: Data Protection & Comparative Frameworks (EU GDPR)
- **Foundations & Comparative Regulatory Models:**
  - Primary EU Law for Data Protection: Charter of Fundamental Rights of the EU (Articles 7 & 8) and Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU, Article 16)
  - Regulatory Approaches: Sectoral Model (United States: HIPAA, COPPA) vs. Comprehensive Omnibus Model (EU GDPR as a global benchmark)
  - Mass Surveillance & Human Rights: *Big Brother Watch and Others v. United Kingdom* (ECtHR 2018/2021)
- **Scope & Applicability of GDPR:**
  - Material Scope & Filing Systems: Applicability to automated and structured manual processing (*Jehovah's Witnesses v. Finland*, CJEU 2018 — door-to-door structured manual collection)
  - Territorial Scope: Article 3(1) r/w Recital 22 (establishment criterion)
  - Extraterritorial Scope: Article 3(2) r/w Recital 23 (targeting criterion: offering goods/services or monitoring behaviour of EU data subjects)
- **Data Concepts, Classifications & Controllership:**
  - Personal Data & Dynamic IP Addresses (*Patrick Breyer v. Bundesrepublik Deutschland*, CJEU 2016)
  - Anonymised Data (excluded from GDPR scope) vs. Pseudonymised Data (Article 4(5) GDPR — subject to GDPR)
  - Joint Data Controllership (*Fashion ID GmbH & Co. KG v. Verbraucherzentrale NRW eV*, CJEU 2019 — Facebook Like button plugin)
- **Core Principles of Data Processing (Article 5 & Recitals):**
  - **Lawfulness, Fairness & Transparency (Art. 5(1)(a)):**
    - Lawfulness: Article 6(1)(a)–(f) lawful grounds for processing (Consent, Contract, Legal Obligation, Vital Interests, Public Task, Legitimate Interests)
    - Fairness: Processing must be fair and equitable in relation to data subjects
    - Transparency: Recital 39 & Article 12 GDPR (transparent information, communication, and modalities)
  - Purpose limitation — Art. 5(1)(b) & Recital 50
  - Data minimisation — Art. 5(1)(c) & Recital 39
  - Data accuracy — Art. 5(1)(d) r/w Art. 16
  - Storage limitation / Retention — Art. 5(1)(e)
  - Integrity & confidentiality (Security) — Art. 5(1)(f) r/w Arts. 32, 33, 34
  - Accountability principle — Art. 5(2)
- **Consent Framework under GDPR:**
  - Definition of Consent: Article 4(11) GDPR
  - Conditions for Consent: Article 7 GDPR
  - Child's Consent: Article 8 GDPR (conditions applicable to child consent in relation to information society services)
  - Prohibition of Forced / Bundled Consent (enforcement actions by DPAs across Austria, Belgium, France, Germany, and Ireland)
- **Sensitive Personal Data:**
  - Article 9 GDPR regime (special categories of personal data)
  - *Bodil Lindqvist* (CJEU, 2004) — Article 8(1) of EU Directive 95/46/EC on processing health-related data
- **Rights of Data Subjects under GDPR:**
  - Right to know (information obligations)
  - Right of access
  - Right to rectification & Right to erasure / "Right to be forgotten" (*Google Spain v. AEPD*)
  - Right to restriction of processing
  - Right to data portability *(Note: omitted in DPDP Act 2023)*
  - Right to object
  - Specific rights relating to automated decision-making / profiling & Right to explanation *(Note: omitted in DPDP Act 2023)*
- **Cross-Border Transfers:** Cross-border data transfers and standard contractual clauses

### Module 6: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 & Rules 2025
- **Legislative History & Constitutional Basis:**
  - Right to Privacy recognized as a Fundamental Right (*K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India*)
  - Justice B.N. Srikrishna Committee Report (2018) & Draft PDP Bill 2018 (primarily modeled on GDPR)
  - Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC) chaired by Meenakshi Lekhi (deliberations on comprehensive governance of personal and non-personal data)
  - Enactment of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
- **Preamble & Scope of the Law:**
  - Preamble: Providing the manner of data processing, recognizing both the right to protect personal data and the need to process personal data for lawful purposes
  - Section 3: Applicability and territorial scope of the Act
- **Select Definitions (Section 2):**
  - Personal Data — Section 2(t): data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to such data
  - Data Principal — Section 2(j)
  - Data Fiduciary — Section 2(i)
  - Data Processor — Section 2(k)
  - Significant Data Fiduciary — Section 2(z)
  - Data Protection Officer — Section 2(l)
  - Consent Manager — Section 2(g)
- **Obligations of Data Fiduciary (Chapter II):**
  - Section 4: Grounds for processing personal data (lawful processing based on Consent and Legitimate Uses)
  - Section 5: Notice requirements
  - Section 6: Consent requirements and validity
- **Rights of Data Principal & Comparative Distinctions (DPDP vs. GDPR):**
  - Rights of Data Principal: Right to access, rectification, erasure, and grievance redressal
  - Right to Nominate (Section 14) — *present in DPDP, absent in GDPR*
  - Consent Manager framework (Sections 2(g) & 6) — *distinct institution in DPDP, absent in GDPR*
  - Rights absent in DPDP: Right to Data Portability and specific rights against Automated Decision-Making
- **Cross-Border Transfers & Implementation:**
  - Transfer of personal data outside India (Section 16)
  - Digital Personal Data Protection Rules 2025

## Landmark Cases
- **Data Protection & Privacy:**
  - *K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India* (Right to Privacy as a Fundamental Right)
  - *Jehovah's Witnesses v. Finland* (CJEU 2018) (Manual collection forming a filing system subject to data protection law)
  - *Patrick Breyer v. Bundesrepublik Deutschland* (CJEU 2016) (Dynamic IP addresses as personal data)
  - *Fashion ID GmbH & Co. KG v. Verbraucherzentrale NRW eV* (CJEU 2019) (Joint data controllership and website plugins)
  - *Bodil Lindqvist* (CJEU 2004) (Sensitive personal health data under EU law)
  - *Google Spain v. AEPD* (Right to be forgotten and erasure)
  - *Schrems v. Data Protection Commissioner* (Schrems I & II — Cross-border data transfers)
  - *Data Protection Commissioner v. Facebook Ireland*
  - *Big Brother Watch and Others v. United Kingdom* (ECtHR 2018/2021) (Bulk interception and privacy rights)
