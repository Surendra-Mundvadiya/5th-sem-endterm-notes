# Other Forms of IP - Course Syllabus

**Course Title:** Other Form IPR Creation and Registration  
**Subject Code:** IP71005  
**Institution:** Rajiv Gandhi School of Intellectual Property Law, IIT Kharagpur  

---

## Course Overview & Objectives
Beyond the traditional pillars of patents, copyrights, and trademarks, allied and para-IPRs (Geographical Indications, Trade Secrets, and Semiconductor Integrated Circuit Layout-Designs) have gained critical strategic importance. This course provides comprehensive theoretical, statutory, comparative, and procedural knowledge regarding their creation, registration, enforcement, and commercial exploitation.

---

## Module 1: Trade Secrets & Undisclosed Information
- **Introduction & Theoretical Framework:**
  - Definition, nature, and legal justification of trade secrets
  - Elements: Commercial value, secrecy, reasonable efforts to maintain secrecy
  - Distinction from patents, copyright, and know-how; advantages and disadvantages
- **Protection of Trade Secrets in Enterprises:**
  - Integration into corporate IP portfolios; physical and technical safeguards
  - Restrictive covenants: Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs), non-compete covenants, Section 27 of Indian Contract Act, 1872 (*Percept D' Mark v. Zaheer Khan*; *Niranjan Shankar Golikari*)
  - Springboard doctrine and inevitable disclosure doctrine
- **International & Comparative Regimes:**
  - Article 39 of TRIPS Agreement; Paris Convention Article 10bis (Unfair Competition)
  - US Regime: Uniform Trade Secrets Act (UTSA), Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA), Economic Espionage Act (EEA)
  - EU Trade Secrets Directive (Directive 2016/943)
- **Trade Secret Protection in India:**
  - Common law remedies (breach of confidence, breach of contract, tort of conversion)
  - Draft National Innovation Bill (2008) and Law Commission recommendations
  - Criminal vs civil remedies, interlocutory injunctions, Anton Piller orders, and damages
- **Commercial Applications:** Trade secrets in competition law, SMEs, joint ventures, and technology licensing

---

## Module 2: Geographical Indications (GI)
- **Conceptual & Historical Foundations:**
  - Definition, economic rationale, and consumer protection rationale
  - Interrelationship and differences between Trademarks and Geographical Indications
  - Collective marks and certification marks vs *sui generis* GI protection
  - Linkage with Traditional Knowledge (TK) and rural development
- **International Framework:**
  - Paris Convention (1883), Madrid Agreement for the Repression of False or Deceptive Indications of Source
  - Lisbon Agreement for the Protection of Appellations of Origin and Geneva Act (2015)
  - TRIPS Agreement: General protection (Article 22) vs additional protection for wines and spirits (Article 23); post-Doha expansion debates
- **Indian Statutory Framework (GI of Goods Act, 1999):**
  - Definitions: Geographical indication, goods, producer, authorized user
  - Registration Procedure: Part A (GI registration) and Part B (Authorized User registration); application, examination, opposition, and entry into register
  - Rights conferred by registration, duration, renewal, and rectification
  - Infringement, passing off, and statutory exceptions
  - Landmark GIs: Darjeeling Tea, Basmati, Kanchipuram Silk, Aranmula Kannadi, Champagne, Scotch Whisky

---

## Module 3: Semiconductor Integrated Circuits Layout-Design
- **Rationale & Technical Foundations:**
  - Technological necessity of protecting layout-designs (topographies) of integrated circuits
  - Limitations of patent and copyright protections for microchip designs
- **International Framework:**
  - Washington Treaty on Intellectual Property in Respect of Integrated Circuits (IPIC Treaty, 1989)
  - TRIPS Agreement provisions (Articles 35–38)
- **Indian Regime (Semiconductor Integrated Circuits Layout-Design Act, 2000):**
  - Criteria for registration: Originality, distinctiveness, not commercially exploited anywhere in the world
  - Prohibition of registration of certain layout-designs
  - Application, examination, opposition, registration, and duration (10 years)
  - Rights of registered proprietor; acts not constituting infringement (reverse engineering, independent creation)
  - Assignment, transmission, and layout-design appellate board
