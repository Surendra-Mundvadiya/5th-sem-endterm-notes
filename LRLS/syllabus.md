# Legal Regulations in Life Sciences - Course Syllabus

**Subject:** Legal Regulations in Life Sciences (LRLS)  
**Semester:** V  
**Institution:** Rajiv Gandhi School of Intellectual Property Law, IIT Kharagpur  

---

## Course Overview & Objectives
This course examines the intersection of law, life sciences, bioethics, and public healthcare policy. It provides an in-depth study of the regulatory governance of biomedical research, clinical trials, pharmaceuticals, medical devices, biotechnology, biosimilars, and intellectual property rights in healthcare under both Indian statutory law and international conventions.

---

## Module 1: Foundations of Bioethics & Biomedical Research
- **Historical Evolution of Bioethics:**
  - Abuses in scientific human experimentation (Tuskegee Syphilis Study, Nazi medical experiments).
  - Landmark International Instruments: Nuremberg Code (1947), Declaration of Helsinki (WMA 1964 and revisions), Belmont Report (1979 - respect for persons, beneficence, justice), CIOMS Guidelines.
- **Indian Ethical Guidelines:**
  - ICMR National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research Involving Human Participants (2017).
  - Principles of Essentiality, Voluntariness, Non-exploitation, Risk Minimization, Confidentiality, and Distributive Justice.

---

## Module 2: Clinical Trials & Human Subject Protection
- **Regulatory Framework in India:**
  - Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945.
  - New Drugs and Clinical Trials (NDCT) Rules, 2019: Clinical trial phases (Phase I to Phase IV), Central Drugs Standard Control Organization (CDSCO), and role of the DCGI.
  - Academic vs commercial clinical trials.
- **Informed Consent & Vulnerable Populations:**
  - Protocol for obtaining valid, voluntary informed consent; audio-visual recording mandates in clinical trials for new chemical entities.
  - Safeguards for vulnerable groups: Children, pregnant women, terminally ill, prisoners, and economically disadvantaged.
- **Institutional Ethics Committees (IEC):**
  - Mandatory registration of Ethics Committees with CDSCO.
  - Composition, quorum, expedited review, monitoring, and conflict of interest policies.
- **Compensation & Serious Adverse Events (SAEs):**
  - SAE reporting timelines and statutory formulas for financial compensation; role of Expert Committees and licensing authorities.

---

## Module 3: Pharmaceutical Regulation & Drugs Control
- **Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940:**
  - Statutory classifications: Drug, Cosmetic, Misbranded drug, Adulterated drug, Spurious drug.
  - Quality standards: Indian Pharmacopoeia (IP) and recognized official pharmacopoeias.
  - Manufacturing, sale, distribution, and import licensing.
  - Drug Inspectors: Powers of search, seizure, inspection, and prosecution under Chapter IV.
- **Compliance & Good Practices:**
  - Good Clinical Practice (GCP), Good Laboratory Practice (GLP), and Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) under Revised Schedule M.
  - Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (PvPI): Post-marketing surveillance and adverse drug reaction reporting.

---

## Module 4: Medical Devices & Recombinant Biotechnology
- **Medical Devices Regulation:**
  - Medical Device Rules (MDR), 2017 (amended 2020): Risk-based classification (Class A, B, C, D).
  - Conformity assessment, manufacturing licenses, clinical performance evaluations, and notified bodies.
- **Biotechnology, Stem Cells & Biosimilars:**
  - Hazardous Microorganisms / Genetically Engineered Organisms Rules (1989).
  - Regulatory hierarchy: Institutional Biosafety Committee (IBSC), Review Committee on Genetic Manipulation (RCGM), Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee (GEAC).
  - National Guidelines for Stem Cell Research (ICMR/DBT).
  - Guidelines on Similar Biologics (Biosimilars): Comparability exercise, quality, safety, and preclinical/clinical data.

---

## Module 5: Patents, Evergreening & Access to Medicines
- **Patentability in Life Sciences:**
  - Section 3(d) of the Patents Act, 1970: Enhanced therapeutic efficacy standard for new forms of known substances; prevention of evergreening.
  - Landmark Precedent: *Novartis AG v. Union of India* (2013) (Glivec / Imatinib Mesylate).
  - Section 3(b), 3(h), 3(i), and 3(j): Inventions contrary to morality, methods of agriculture, diagnostic/therapeutic methods, and plants/animals.
- **Compulsory Licensing & Public Health:**
  - TRIPS Agreement and Doha Declaration on TRIPS and Public Health (2001).
  - Section 84 Compulsory Licensing: Affordable pricing, reasonable requirements of the public (*Natco Pharma v. Bayer Corp* - Nexavar).
  - Emergency licensing under Section 92 and export licensing under Section 92A.
