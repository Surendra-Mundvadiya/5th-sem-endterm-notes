# International Investment Law - Course Syllabus

**Semester:** V  
**Institution:** Rajiv Gandhi School of Intellectual Property Law, IIT Kharagpur  

---

## Course Overview & Objectives
This course provides a comprehensive analysis of the legal principles, bilateral investment treaties (BITs), and international arbitral mechanisms governing foreign direct and portfolio investments. The curriculum examines the evolution from customary international law and diplomatic protection to modern Investor-State Dispute Settlement (ISDS), substantive investor rights, host state regulatory sovereignty, and international arbitral jurisprudence under the ICSID Convention and UNCITRAL rules.

---

## Module 1: Foundations & Historical Evolution of Investment Protection
- **Historical Context:** Early foreign commerce, concession agreements, and colonial trade monopolies (e.g., East India Company); economic drivers of cross-border capital flows (capital-exporting vs. capital-importing states).
- **Customary International Law & State Responsibility:**
  - Doctrine of Diplomatic Protection: State right vs. individual right, continuous nationality rule, exhaustion of local remedies (ELR).
  - Classical Doctrines: Calvo Doctrine (domestic court jurisdiction & strict sovereignty) vs. Hull Doctrine (prompt, adequate, and effective compensation).
- **Transition to Treaty-Based Protection:** Friendship, Commerce, and Navigation (FCN) treaties; first Bilateral Investment Treaty (Germany-Pakistan, 1959); global BIT expansion.

---

## Module 2: Scope of Application & Definitional Thresholds
- **Definition and Scope of "Investment":**
  - Asset-based vs enterprise-based definitions in international treaties.
  - Speculative flows vs genuine Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) & portfolio investments.
  - The *Salini* Test under ICSID Article 25(1): Substantial capital contribution, duration of project, participation in operational/commercial risks, and contribution to host state's economic development.
- **Definition and Nationality of "Investor":**
  - Natural persons: Dual nationality principles and effective/dominant nationality.
  - Legal entities: Place of incorporation (*siège social*) vs principal place of business; piercing the corporate veil, shell companies, and treaty shopping.
  - Denial of benefits clauses in contemporary investment treaties.

---

## Module 3: Substantive Standards of Investor Protection
- **Relative Standards of Treatment:**
  - **National Treatment (NT):** Non-discrimination principle; the "like circumstances / like situations" test.
  - **Most-Favoured-Nation (MFN) Treatment:** Extension of third-party treaty benefits; substantive provisions vs dispute settlement mechanisms (*Maffezini* doctrine).
- **Absolute Standards of Treatment:**
  - **Fair and Equitable Treatment (FET):** Minimum Standard of Treatment (MST) under customary international law vs autonomous treaty standards; legitimate expectations, due process, and non-arbitrariness (*Neer*, *Waste Management II*, *Tecmed*, *CMS*).
  - **Full Protection and Security (FPS):** Due diligence obligation; physical security and extension to legal/regulatory security.
- **Expropriation and Compensation:**
  - Direct vs Indirect Expropriation (measures tantamount to expropriation, creeping expropriation).
  - Regulatory police powers vs compensable expropriation.
  - Lawful expropriation criteria: Public purpose, non-discrimination, due process, and compensation payment.

---

## Module 4: Investor-State Dispute Settlement (ISDS) & Arbitral Framework
- **Arbitral Fora & Procedural Rules:**
  - International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID): Jurisdiction *ratione materiae*, *personae*, and *voluntatis* (Article 25); self-contained arbitral regime.
  - UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules and Model Law; ad-hoc tribunals vs institutional mechanisms.
- **Recognition, Enforcement, and Challenge of Awards:**
  - ICSID Article 54 (direct enforcement as final domestic judgment) and Article 52 annulment grounds.
  - Non-ICSID awards under the New York Convention 1958 (Article V public policy exceptions).
- **Transparency & Systemic Reform:**
  - UNCITRAL Rules on Transparency & Mauritius Convention.
  - Contemporary critiques of ISDS: Regulatory chill, third-party funding, conflicts of interest.
  - Reform trajectories: Multilateral Investment Court (MIC), standing appellate bodies, and India's Model BIT (2016).
